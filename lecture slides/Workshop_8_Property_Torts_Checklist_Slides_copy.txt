This is not intended to replace the lecture slides but 
is meant to be a supplemental, to give you another 
view, and to provide a structured way of thinking 
about private nuisance and Rylands. 
 The private nuisance slides that follow are based 
around the model set out by the Supreme Court in 
Fearn v Tate. The idea of ‘common and ordinary 
use’ in Fearn does not replace the old idea of 
‘reasonableness’. Instead, it is just describing the 
same old ideas in different language. 
LAW OF TORT
Private Nuisance 1
i.
C can sue if exclusive possession (Hunter)
ii.
Actionable damage if property damage, encroachment, or repeated interference with 
amenity value (Hunter; Halsey;  Williams)
iii.
Substantial interference with C’s common and ordinary use of the land? (Fearn)
a) Substantial interference: with normal living, not ‘dainty modes of living’ (Walter) 
+ more than a ‘trifling inconvenience’ (St Helens Smelting) 
b) Ordinary use: ‘the ordinary use of property for the purposes of residence’ not a 
‘sensitive user’ (Robinson; Morris)
iv.
D has a defence if their use is common and ordinary and conveniently (Fearn)
a) Locality [but not for property damage] (Sturges; Coventry; St Helen’s Smelting)
b) Intensity (Kennaway)
c) Duration/extent 
d) Not malicious (Christie)

v.
Normal causation rules
vi. Normal defences + defence of prescription (Coventry)
vii. Remedy. Injunction unless Shelfer criteria met:
a) Injury to C’s rights is small
b) Injury is capable of being estimated in money
c) Injury can be adequately compensated by a small
