Direct Interference
• For example: 
• throwing objects onto a 
neighbour’s land; 
• sitting on a wall; 
• stepping over a boundary;
• mining...
Key: Distinguishable from indirect 
interference (nuisance). 
Indirect interference examples = 
noise; smells; other interferences with 
someone’s use and enjoyment of their 
property.
Intention
• Intention to make contact
You do not need:
• intention to trespass; or
• intention to cause harm
League Against Cruel Sports v Scott [1986] QB 240: 
‘the master will be liable for trespass if he intended 
to cause hounds to enter such land, or if by his 
failure to exercise proper control over them he 
caused them to enter such land.’ (at 252 per Park J)
Defences & Remedies to Trespass 
to Land
• Defences
o Permission (License or Consent)
o Legal justification (Jeffrey v Black [1978] Q.B. 490 – trespass ab initio)
o Necessity (Esso Petroleum Co Ltd v Southport Corp [1956] A.C. 218 and Rigby v 
Chief Constable of Northamptonshire [1985] 1 W.L.R. 1242)
N.B. It is no defence to argue mistaken belief (even if reasonable).
• Remedies
o Damages (nominal where no damage)
o Injunction
