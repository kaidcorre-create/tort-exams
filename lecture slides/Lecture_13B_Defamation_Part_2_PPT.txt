Elements of a defamation action
(Defamation Act + precedent) 
(1) defamatory statement
(‘right-thinking members of [this] society 
generally’)
(2) statement must be such that it  has caused/ is 
likely to cause serious harm to reputation OR, in 
the case of a trading for profit, has caused or likely to 
cause serious financial loss (see s 1 Defamation Act 
2013)
(3) reference to that other person
(4) publication
‘a threshold requirement of seriousness’

Defence of Honest Comment* 
Complete defence to a defamation action
[Formerly] referred to as ‘fair comment’
Developed under the common law
Now given a statutory footing under s 3 Defamation Act 
2013:
1) statement of opinion (honestly even if unreasonably 
held);
2) basis of opinion indicated generally or specifically; 
and 
3) opinion held on any fact at the time of statement.

Offer to Make Amends
Defamation Act 1996, s.3:
In writing;
Expressed to make amends under s. 2 (in relation 
to the specific defamatory meaning);
Must state whether it is a qualified offer.
Must suitably correct the statement and offer a 
sufÏcient apology; publish reasonably; pay any 
agreed amounts. (s. 4)
  Publication on a matter of public interest
• S 4 Defamation Act provides a defence for statements which 
are either in the public interest or which the defendant 
reasonably believed were in the public interest.
• The existence of this defence can be traced to the House of 
Lords judgment in Reynolds v Times Newspapers Ltd [2001] 2 
AC 127 
• 10 factors: seriousness; nature of information and extent of 
public concern; source; steps to verify; status of the 
information (authoritative?); urgency; comment sought from 
claimant?; claimant’s side of story?; tone of article; 
circumstances and timing of the article.

reason to believe, that what he did caused or

Ngo v The Guardian & New Media


End of last year on preliminary issues:

a. The natural and ordinary meaning of the words complained of in the Article;

b. Any innuendo meaning of the words complained of (assuming without deciding 
that the fact pleaded in the first sentence of paragraph 6.1 of the Particulars of 
Claim is a fact known to at least one reader of the Article);

c. Whether the words complained of were defamatory of the Claimant at common 
law;

d. Whether the words complained of are (or include) an expression of opinion; and

e. If the words complained of were (or include) an expression of opinion, whether 
the basis of that opinion was indicated, in general or specific terms, for the 
purposes of s.3(3) of the Defamation Act 2013.
