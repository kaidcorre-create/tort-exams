Working deﬁnitions of Tort
“A tort is a species of wrong. A wrong is a breach of a duty 
owed to someone else. A breach of a duty owed to 
someone else is an infringement of a right they have 
against the tortfeasor.” (R Stevens)
“In tort law, a claimant, C, will have a cause of action if 
either (i) she has had her rights wrongly infringed or (ii) 
she has incurred a non-trivial loss, injury or damage that 
was caused by certain wrongful conduct on the part of a 
defendant, D.” (J Murphy)
LAW OF TORT
Tort v the other stuﬀ
Tort v Criminal
• Overlap (e.g. assault and battery)
• Ingredients of a crime v elements of a tort
• Compensation not sanction
• Diﬀerent courts, burdens of proof, etc.
Tort v Contract
• Overlap (e.g. concurrent liability)
• Source of duty: ‘general’ not ‘special’ rights
• Content of duty: ﬁxed by law not contract
• Aims: compensation not protecting expectations

LAW OF TORT
Why bother with theories? 
“[W]e deepen our understanding when we learn that 
disparate phenomena are linked together. In physics, for 
example, the theory of thermodynamics showed us the 
relationship between mechanics and heat. In economics, the 
theory of the ﬁrm showed us that, across industries that look 
nothing alike, a simple principle helps explain the 
organization of economic activity. … [A]bsent such a theory, 
our understanding of the way the world works seems 
incomplete.” (S Hershovitz)
LAW OF TORT
Theories with diﬀerent aims
Interpretive theories
• Does the theory ﬁt with and justify the body of law?
• Does the theory have ‘explanatory power’?
• Allows us to understand what the law is, to predict developments, and to assess how far a 
body of law is coherent
Prescriptive theories 
• Is the theory internally coherent?
• Does the theory ﬁt with our basic moral intuitions?
• Can the theory achieve its aims in practice?
• Allows us to argue rationally about what the law ought to be

Corrective Justice
“Individuals who are responsible for the wrongful losses 
of others have a duty to repair the loss”. (J Coleman)
“Corrective justice is … an interpersonal or bipolar justice. 
Questions of corrective justice are answered, not by 
looking at individuals in isolation or at societies as a 
whole, but by examining interactions between 
individuals.” (A Beever)
‘Relational wrongs’
LAW OF TORT
Rights Theory
“Before a defendant can be characterized as a tortfeasor the 
anterior question of whether the claimant had a right against 
him must be answered. The law of torts is concerned with 
the secondary obligations generated by the infringement of 
primary rights. The infringement of rights, not the inﬂiction 
of loss, is the gist of the law of torts. This conception of the 
law of torts I shall call the ‘rights model’.” (R Stevens)
LAW OF TORT
Compensation & Deterrence 
Not about justice between individuals, but rather, 
achieving policy goals for society as a whole
Deterrence of antisocial conduct, and compensation for 
those who have been injured
