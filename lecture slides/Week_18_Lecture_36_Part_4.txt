Problem Question 1 - Answer
X, the driver of the Defendant’s (D) petrol tanker lit a cigarette with a match and threw 
the match on the floor where petrol from the tanker was being transferred to petrol 
pumps owned by the Claimant (C). As a result of X’s negligence there was an explosion 
causing damage to C’s property.
Is D liable?
•
X’s negligence already identified
•
Is D in breach of a non-delegable duty of 
care? 
o Provision of competent staff?
•
Is D vicariously liable for X?
o In the course of X’s employment?
o Close connection?
Century Insurance Co Ltd v Northern Ireland Road 
Transport Board [1942] AC 509, 519
‘The duty of the workman to his employer is so to 
conduct himself in doing his work as not negligently 
to cause damage either to the employer himself or 
his property or to third persons or their property, and 
thus to impose the same liability on the employer as 
if he had been doing the work himself and committed 
the negligent act.’
D held vicariously liable.

Scenario 1
Sheila and Anton work for Bicycle Plus Limited 
(BPL). They had spent the preceding day and 
evening in lengthy meetings at a client’s ofÏces

were in Sheila’s car which was involved in an 
accident for which she was admittedly 
negligent. Anton was injured. 
Is BPL liable?
Smith v Stages [1989] AC 928, 956
‘The paramount rule is that an employee travelling 
on the highway will be acting in the course of his 
employment if, and only if, he is at the material time 
going about his employer's business. One must not 
confuse the duty to turn up for one's work with the 
concept of already being "on duty" while travelling to 
it.’

Problem Question 3 – spot the 
difference - Answer
Scenario 1
Nina works for Bluepool Horse Taxi Services (BHTS) 
driving a horse and cart used often by tourists in the 
town of Bluepool. She is told she is not to leave the 
horse and cart unattended at any time and when 
going for lunch should return to BHTS’ warehouse. 
On leaving her horse and cart unattended to grab a 
bite to eat at a local restaurant, the horses spooked 
due to a car backfire, broke their tether, and charged 
into the road causing several cars to collide and 
some pedestrians to be injured. 
Is BHTS liable?
Scenario 2
Nina works for Bluepool Horse Taxi Services (BHTS) 
driving a horse and cart for their new novel delivery 
service in the town of Bluepool. On the way back to 
BHTS’ warehouse following completion of a delivery, 
another member of staff with her, Marissa, 
persuaded Nina to take her to her mum’s house on 
the other side of town to show her the horses. On 
the way to Marissa’s mum’s house, and as a result of 
Nina’s negligence, they were involved in an accident 
with a pedestrian who was injured.
Is BHTS liable?
Whatman v Pearson (1867) LR 3 CP 422 – liable 
Storey v Ashton (1868) LR 4 QB 476 – not liable
Applying Joel v Morison (1834) 172 ER 1338 – ‘If he was going out of his way, against his master's implied commands, when 
driving on his master's business, he will make his master liable; but if he was going on a frolic of his own, without being at all 
on his master's business, the master will not be liable.’
